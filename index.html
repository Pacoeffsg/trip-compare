The one number that decides this trip

A design-led room runs roughly double an ordinary good one — around $400–500 a night against $200–250 in most of these places, and more in Japan. Over eleven nights that difference is about $2,500, which is a quarter of the entire budget. Nowhere with an expensive flight survives paying it every night.

Which is why split stay is the setting that matters. Four nights in the building you actually came for, the rest in something clean and well-located, is how you get a Barrocal or an Argos into a $10k trip. Put the signature nights in the middle, not at the start — you'll be too jet-lagged to look at anything properly on night one.

Your dates still carry three complications in Japan: Silver Week runs 19–23 September, the first full one since 2015, and your last five nights sit inside it. The Asian Games open in Aichi–Nagoya on 19 September. And mid-September is peak typhoon season in Okinawa, which averages about 7.7 approaches a year against three or four for the mainland.

Where the numbers come from

  • The links — each property links to a name search, a Booking.com results page with 12–23 September and two guests already filled in, and a map pin. They're built from the property name rather than saved deep links, so they can't rot or point somewhere stale — but always confirm you've landed on the right property before paying, especially for the smaller places where names repeat.
  • Rooms — signature rates are the published or recently observed nightly price for the named properties — Argos in Cappadocia from roughly US$273–330, Hoshinoya Taketomi across a 2026 band of about US$268–871 with US$746 seen mid-year, and comparable figures elsewhere. Ordinary rates now reflect whole-place rentals and family guesthouses rather than hotels, which is why most totals fell against earlier versions — self-catering also quietly removes a chunk of the daily food line in the expensive destinations. Tokyo's citywide average ran about ¥33,000 in May 2026, up nearly 13% year on year.
  • Flights — shortest routing only. Every fare here assumes a direct service where one exists, and a single stop otherwise. No two- and three-stop budget routings are priced, which raises several numbers against earlier versions: Okinawa moves from a $852 Scoot itinerary via Taipei to about $1,600 through Tokyo, and Athens from a sub-$1,300 27-hour Scoot routing to about $2,200 on a Gulf carrier. Albania is the one destination with no single-stop option at all, and is priced and flagged as two stops. Every card links straight to Google Flights for SYD to that airport, with 12–23 September and two adults already in the query — direct-only where a nonstop exists. Underlying anchors, quoted for these dates in early August 2026: Sydney–Nadi from about $386 return for 13–22 September; Sydney–Okinawa around $852 via Taipei; JAL Sydney–Tokyo from about $2,033 for September. Europe sits in a $1,800–2,500 shoulder band, with sub-$1,300 fares available on 27–43 hour routings.
  • Getting around inside the country — costed as its own line rather than lumped into a general allowance: domestic flights, trains, ferries, rental cars with fuel, and airport transfers, priced for two. Earlier versions bundled this with activities in a single figure that was, in several cases, smaller than the listed activities alone. The Rarotonga–Aitutaki hop is about $900 for two on its own; eleven days of car in British Columbia is roughly $1,750 with fuel and the island ferry.
  • Activities — the sum of the excursions listed on each card, so the number you see is the number you're being charged for. Anything you skip comes straight back off the total.
  • Daily spend — food, city transit, entries and incidentals per person per day. City metros and taxis sit here; anything between cities sits in the line above.
  • Buffer — insurance at roughly $90 a head plus 10% contingency. In a typhoon window that contingency is the whole point.

Planning estimates, not quotes. Rates move daily and September 2026 has two demand events stacked into it — price the shortlist properly, and read the cancellation terms twice on anything south of Kyushu.
