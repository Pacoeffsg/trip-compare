Nightly rates are for two, typical September, before any deal. Architecture and heat are scored one to five.

The one number that decides this trip

A design-led room runs roughly double an ordinary good one — around $400–500 a night against $200–250 in most of these places, and more in Japan. Over eleven nights that difference is about $2,500, which is a quarter of the entire budget. Nowhere with an expensive flight survives paying it every night.

Which is why split stay is the setting that matters. Four nights in the building you actually came for, the rest in something clean and well-located, is how you get an Alila or an Argos into a $10k trip. Put the signature nights in the middle, not at the start — you'll be too jet-lagged to look at anything properly on night one.

Your dates still carry three complications in Japan: Silver Week runs 19–23 September, the first full one since 2015, and your last five nights sit inside it. The Asian Games open in Aichi–Nagoya on 19 September. And mid-September is peak typhoon season in Okinawa, which averages about 7.7 approaches a year against three or four for the mainland.

Where the numbers come from

  • Rooms — signature rates are the published or recently observed nightly price for the named properties: Alila Villas Uluwatu has shown around US$692 a night, Argos in Cappadocia from roughly US$273–330, Hoshinoya Taketomi across a 2026 band of about US$268–871 with US$746 seen mid-year. Ordinary-good rates use each destination's typical well-reviewed four-star. Tokyo's citywide average ran about ¥33,000 in May 2026, up nearly 13% year on year.
  • Flights — anchored to fares quoted for these dates in early August 2026: Sydney–Nadi from about $386 return for 13–22 September; Sydney–Okinawa around $852 via Taipei; JAL Sydney–Tokyo from about $2,033 for September. Europe sits in a $1,800–2,500 shoulder band, with sub-$1,300 fares available on 27–43 hour routings.
  • Daily spend — food, local transport, entries and incidentals per person per day, at the level each place genuinely costs.
  • Buffer — insurance at roughly $90 a head plus 10% contingency. In a typhoon window that contingency is the whole point.

Planning estimates, not quotes. Rates move daily and September 2026 has two demand events stacked into it — price the shortlist properly, and read the cancellation terms twice on anything south of Kyushu.
